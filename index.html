The puzzle

One building, two lives

  • At NU, studying and living share one building: integration by design.
  • When what people want doesn't match their environment, it causes strain and conflict. Kulka, 1979, as cited in Kreiner et al., 2009
  • So how do students draw, or drop, a line the building doesn't draw for them?

02 / Where I stand

Interpretivism

I ask what coffee means to students, not how much they drink.

  • My question is a meaning question.
  • Students create the boundary, so their words are my evidence.
  • No IV/DV. No counting cups.

Schwartz-Shea & Yanow, 2012

04 / What others found · 1 of 2

A common drink…

  1. 1

    Coffee is everywhere

    Over 90% of students consume caffeine. Coffee is the main source. Mahoney et al., 2019; Riera-Sampol et al., 2022

  2. 2

    It plays a dual role

    For grades and jobs Franke et al., 2012 and for taste and friends. Mahoney et al., 2019

  3. 3

    The pivot

    Studying and sociability are studied separately, never coffee as a way to move between them.

04 / What others found · 2 of 2

…and an open question

  1. 4

    Life domains sit on a continuum

    Segmented: hard to cross. Integrated: easy to cross, hard to mark. Ashforth et al., 2000

  2. 5

    Boundary work has forms

    Behavioral, temporal, physical, communicative. Objects can be markers, like a “collar”. Kreiner et al., 2009

  3. 6

    The gap

    Only work and home. Not students who live and study in one place. Not coffee. Not Kazakhstan.

  4. ?

    So: how do NU students use coffee to mark, cross, or blur that boundary?

05 / What I expect, and could be wrong

Expectations

  1. 1Coffee may carry several tactics at once.
  2. 2It may mark, cross, or blur, or do more than one.
  3. 3Coffee may start studying, but something else may end it.
  4. 4Some just drink it for pleasure, with no boundary.

05 / Positionality

I'm a partial insider

What it gives me

  • I know NU's places.
  • I study at NU but live in the city, so I notice what residents take for granted.
  • As a coffee lover, I notice how others see it.

What it may miss

  • I haven't lived the overlap myself.
  • I may overrate coffee.
  • Peers may tell me what I want to hear.
  • NU may be less integrated than I think.
